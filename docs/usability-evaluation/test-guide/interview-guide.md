# Interview guide

Related issue: MET-A-027 (#26)

The interviews are semi-structured. The questions are used as
a guide, but we also ask follow-up questions based on the
participants' answers and what happens during the test.

The interviews are conducted in Danish. The questions below
have been translated into English for the repository.

## Introduction

Thank the participant for taking part and explain the purpose
of the test.

Make sure they understand that we are testing the system,
not their ability to do their job.

Ask for consent to:

- Observe the participant.
- Take notes during the test.
- Record the interviews and test sessions.

Encourage the participant to think aloud and let us know if
anything is unclear or does not behave as expected.

## Supplementary questions (both roles)

These questions are documented in the operator interview
notes and supplement the role-specific guide.

### Before the test

- What tools and communication methods do you currently use?
- What works particularly well about the radio?
- What do you find most difficult about the radio or other tools?
- What helps when a situation does not follow the usual procedure?
- What information or functionality do you miss in your current tools?

### After the test

- What is your overall impression of the application?
- Which parts did you like or dislike?
- What would you change about the screens or functionality, and why?
- What did you find most difficult?

## Steward interview

### Before the test

The purpose of the before-interview is to understand their
current way of working before introducing our solution.

1. How do you normally receive information when a fallback
   scenario is activated?
2. How do you find out which station you need to go to and
   what you need to do there?
3. What information do you need most when a scenario starts?
4. Are there parts of your current workflow where you
   experience uncertainty, waiting or other difficulties?

Additional topics to ask about:

- The tools they currently use.
- Communication with the control room.
- Communication between stewards.
- What works well in the current process.
- What they do when a situation does not follow the usual
  procedure.
- Information or functionality they currently miss.

### During the test

Ask the participant to think aloud.

If they become quiet, ask what they are looking for,
what they are thinking or why they chose a particular action.

Avoid telling them how to complete the task unless they
are unable to continue.

After each task, ask:

1. How was it to complete the task?
2. Was there anything you had difficulty finding or understanding?
3. How could you tell that your action had been registered?
4. Did anything happen differently from what you expected?

Where relevant, ask what the colours, numbers and symbols
mean to them.

Ask them to rate the difficulty of the task:

| Rating | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Very easy |
| 2 | Easy |
| 3 | Neither easy nor difficult |
| 4 | Difficult |
| 5 | Very difficult |

### After the test

1. How was using the application compared to the way you
   normally receive information and report your status?
2. Was there any information missing?
3. Did anything require more steps or attention than expected?
4. How would the application fit into your work during a
   fallback scenario?
5. If you could change one thing, what would it be?
6. Is there anything about your daily work that we have not
   asked about?

Ask follow-up questions about problems observed during
the tasks and any suggestions the participant brings up.

## Operator interview

### Before the test

1. What do you normally do in the control room when a
   fallback scenario is activated?
2. How do you currently keep track of where stewards are
   and which stations need coverage?
3. What information do you need to find quickly to
   coordinate the situation?
4. How do you communicate changes to stewards and make
   sure the information has been received?
5. How do you determine when a fallback scenario needs
   to be activated?
6. When did you last experience a situation where the
   current tools or procedures did not work as expected?
7. What happened, and how did you resolve the situation?

Additional topics:

- Current tools used in the control room.
- Scenario selection and activation.
- Communication and coordination with stewards.
- Information that is difficult to keep track of.
- Problems with the current workflow.

### During the test

Use the same general task questions and difficulty scale
as for the steward tests.

Pay particular attention to how the operator interprets
station coverage, scenario changes and communication
with stewards.

### After the test

1. How did the overview compare to the tools you use today?
2. How was it to find stations that needed coverage and
   follow the stewards' statuses?
3. How was it to handle scenario changes and see how
   they affected staffing?
4. Was there any information or functionality missing?
5. How would the system fit into the control room's
   workflow during a fallback scenario?
6. What would you change first, and is there anything
   else we should take into consideration?

## Interview notes

Record suggestions from the participants separately from
suggestions introduced by the interviewers.

If a participant agrees with a suggestion we introduce,
this should not automatically be documented as something
they independently requested.
