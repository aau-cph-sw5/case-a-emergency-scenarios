## What went well

**Improved communication and coordination**

Communication has continued to improve throughout the sprints, both within the individual groups and across the integration team. 
Issues that previously caused significant delays have gradually become smaller obstacles that are easier to identify and resolve.

PR reviews and communication between developers have become faster, and people are increasingly comfortable reaching out directly when they need clarification or assistance.

**Stronger shared understanding of the system**

The teams now have a much clearer understanding of the overall product, the intended functionality and the dependencies between different parts of the system.

Earlier in the project, people were more dependent on having specific responsibilities assigned to them. 
We now have a stronger shared mental model of the system, which allows developers to work more independently while still understanding how their work affects the other groups.

This has also made it easier to identify dependencies and determine which work needs to be coordinated before implementation.

**Increased ownership and initiative**

There has been a noticeable improvement in how people take responsibility for the project.

Team members are more confident in identifying issues, suggesting changes, refining PBIs and taking initiative without waiting for explicit instructions. We have become more comfortable discussing whether existing PBIs should be changed, merged or split into smaller issues when that better reflects the actual work.

This has helped us move away from simply following the initial backlog towards actively taking responsibility for the quality and direction of the product.

**Integration NEWS and transparency**

The Integration NEWS updates have worked well as a way of keeping everyone informed about relevant changes, decisions and ongoing work.

Even when developers were not directly involved in integration, they could trust that communication between the groups was being handled and that dependencies or conflicts were being discussed.

This has increased confidence in the integration team and improved trust between the groups. Communication feels more open, and we increasingly work as one larger team rather than three separate groups contributing to the same repository.

**A more stable product**

The system has reached a point where much of the remaining work is focused on refinement and improving the quality of existing functionality rather than implementing the basic flows from scratch.

This has allowed us to spend more time considering usability, technical improvements and how the different features fit together.

## What did not

**The overall Scrum Master role did not work as intended**

We experimented with having an overall Scrum Master within the integration team. However, in practice the role functioned more like an additional integration team member than a distinct Scrum Master.

The responsibilities and expectations of the role were not sufficiently clear, and the team did not consistently rely on the role to facilitate or coordinate work.

Instead, responsibilities were distributed more organically between members based on their knowledge, experience and confidence with the tasks.

During the retrospective, we discussed whether this was necessarily negative. 
Theo and Christian pointed out that strictly assigning roles can sometimes force people into responsibilities that do not reflect how they work best.

Our experience suggests that allowing responsibilities to develop organically can support initiative and collaboration. 
However, it is still important that the necessary responsibilities are actually covered.

The fact that someone outside the integration team did not remember that we had an overall Scrum Master also indicates that the role was not sufficiently visible or established.

**Meeting transcription**

The transcription approach did not work as expected, particularly because Danish speech was not transcribed reliably.
This limited its usefulness as documentation and meant that we could not depend on automatic transcription to accurately capture discussions.

**Keeping documentation aligned with refinement**

During the refinement of MET-A-018 and MET-A-019, some acceptance criteria were changed or clarified.

This raised the question of whether the semester documentation should also be updated whenever the backlog changes.

The general agreement was that relevant documentation should be kept aligned where practical, particularly when the changes are significant enough to affect how the requirement or solution is understood.

## Where we lost time

**Unclear role responsibilities**

Some time and coordination effort was lost because the overall Scrum Master role did not have clearly defined responsibilities.

Although the team managed to distribute the work, this meant that the role itself did not provide the intended structure or additional support.

**Refinement and documentation changes**

Changes to acceptance criteria and the scope of PBIs required additional clarification and coordination.

MET-A-019 is an example where the remaining comparison and evaluation requirements needed further discussion to determine the appropriate level of work.

**Cross-team integration and communication**

Although communication improved significantly, work affecting shared environments still requires closer coordination.

We experienced cases where changes were merged into staging without sufficient communication to the other groups. 
This makes it harder for everyone to maintain a clear understanding of the current state of the system.

**Transcription limitations**

The unsuccessful Danish transcription also meant that discussions could not be documented as efficiently as intended.

**Coordination of shared work**

It is important that changes affecting shared environments, contracts or other groups are communicated before or when they are integrated.

The Integration NEWS updates should continue, since they have made it easier for everyone to follow the progress and decisions made across the groups.

**Shared report**

The common report will be distributed through Trello. Each group selects the sections they will take responsibility for and identifies the specific authors in the comments so that an overview of who did what remains clear.
Sections should subsequently be reviewed by someone from another group to improve consistency and ensure that the report is understandable to readers who were not involved in writing the individual sections.

**Reflecting on our Nexus implementation**

The difference between the formal Nexus and Scrum roles and how we actually worked should be addressed in the final report.

We should explain the intended responsibilities, how they changed in practice, and why we made those adjustments.

Rather than only describing that we used Nexus, the report should demonstrate how we continuously adapted the framework based on the needs and experience of the teams.

## One improvement for next sprint

**Improvement.**

Make communication about changes to shared environments, contracts and cross-group dependencies an explicit part of integration coordination.

Before changes affecting other groups are merged or introduced, the relevant groups should be informed. 
The integration team should also maintain an overview of these changes through its existing communication channels.
